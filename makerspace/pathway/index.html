Venture building

The venture pathway

The DreamSpace Lifecycle (three stages, one thread and four support modes) for changemakers who choose the venture route.

Three stages, one thread, four support modes

The DreamSpace Lifecycle is DreamSpace Academy’s pathway for building impact ventures. It picks up where the Maker Journey ends, and only for the people who take the venture route, the other routes out of the Journey do not pass through here, and are not lesser for it.

Why this is drawn as three and not five

The pathway used to be described as five sequential stages: discovery, challenge-based learning, grassroots innovation, domain expertise, and venture building. Reviewing the documented cases, almost none of them ran those five in order, and two of the five turned out not to be stages at all.

Challenge-based learning is a method, and methods run through stages rather than occupying one. Domain expertise is something a changemaker draws on when they need it, at any point, sometimes without ever passing through a formal programme. Both were moved to where the record says they actually sit. The five names are kept; the numbered sequence is not.

Two ways in

Discovered

Outreach, bootcamps, exposure events, discovery workshops. This is the historical route, and it worked, but it depended on finding people who arrived already motivated. That is a search problem, and search problems do not scale predictably.

Co-created

The Maker Journey builds the motivation rather than hunting for it. Explorer, Maker and Innovator produce people who have already chosen to work on something real. This is current practice, and it converts discovery from a search problem into a delivery problem.

The hardest constraint was never equipment, funding or curriculum: it was identifying changemakers, and not because reaching people was hard. Intake through field visits, open application calls and bootcamps already worked at volume. What was hard was waiting for already-motivated individuals to surface, which makes supply unpredictable and exhausting to sustain.

There is also a rarer third variety, recorded separately because it is genuinely different: scouted, where someone is found through work they had already published themselves, without ever attending anything. It is real, it is not a sub-case of discovery, and it is not evidenced at scale. It is the same thing the grassroots-innovation literature describes when networks scout existing unaided innovators[47], which makes this model a hybrid: mostly creating grassroots innovators, occasionally finding them.

A person entering this pathway is a Changemaker Candidate, entered on demonstrated motivation and commitment, not yet a Changemaker.

The gate, grassroots innovation

A Changemaker Candidate becomes a Changemaker by producing a grassroots innovation. Not by time served, not by completing a programme, not by intent.

The output can be a product, a service or an initiative, the form does not matter. What matters is that all three tests hold: the problem came from the community, a user outside the makerspace has tested it, and someone depends on the outcome.

What the gate does

It excludes work built around a set problem. When past projects were screened against these tests, the largest single cause of failure was the first one. The problem had come from a competition brief, a funder’s call, a lab exercise or the maker’s own curiosity rather than from a community. That is what the first test exists to catch, including when the build itself was excellent. It is not a test of who introduced the problem: mentoring and lab support are the normal route here and do not affect the gate.

It makes the model’s central number countable. With a gate, “how many changemakers” has a defensible answer. Without one, the number is a headcount.

This is the best-evidenced part of the model and, by the record, its rarest stage. The destination is better documented than the gate, which points at where the work now sits.

Impact-venture building

For Changemakers who take the venture route, the third stage is launching and growing something that sustains itself and has a purpose beyond itself. Rather than running every founder through one programme, specific programmes are designed for this stage, a startup challenge with seed funding is one such vehicle.

Progression here is measured by the venture operating, and by revenue and impact being tracked, not by a graduation date. Ventures are reported as active, dormant, closed or transformed, separately, because a portfolio that only lists the live ones is not a portfolio.

This stage is where the “Earn” hypothesis is tested. It is not where it is asserted.

The thread, challenge-based learning

Challenge-based learning is how teaching runs at every point on this pathway, not a phase of it[12]. Lab rotations, interdisciplinary workshops and challenge projects appear before discovery, during innovation, and inside venture building.

Artificial intelligence gets the same treatment for the same reason. It runs through the whole pathway rather than sitting in one stage or in a space of its own. Anything that shows up in every kind of work is a thread, and drawing it as a stage misdescribes the work.

The four support modes

These are offered in any combination and any order, before or after the gate. The record shows nobody using them as a sequence.

ModeWhat is provided
Co-creationBuilding alongside the changemaker, co-founding, joint projects, shared risk. Not building it for them.
BridgingIntroductions to funders, specialists, networks and international opportunities.
Domain expertiseSpecialists, including international ones, brought in to refine a solution when the work needs it.
FundingSeed grants, an impact loan facility, and help getting in front of the people who fund things.
Bridging has no external literature

Bridging is the mode most often named as what makes this organisation different, and it has essentially no external research literature behind it. A search across a corpus of nearly three hundred papers in the field returned nothing under any of six terms: broker, brokering, knowledge broker, intermediary, boundary spanning and translation. Those were the words used at the time, and they are reported here as typed. The corpus has not yet been searched under bridging or linking social capital, so the zero is not final.

Either way, the academy’s own record is for now the only evidence there will be, so bridging is the first thing instrumented. See measurement.

The measure this makes possible

Because the gate is explicit, a genuine conversion measure falls out of it: candidates in, Changemakers out. That denominator is the reason the gate was defined precisely.

Two measures are published rather than one, because they answer different questions and collapsing them produces a number that can exceed 100%:

  • Candidate conversion, candidates meeting the Changemaker gate, divided by eligible candidates. A measure about people.
  • Innovation yield, qualifying innovations, divided by candidates, cohort or person-time. A measure about output.

Both require stable identifiers for people and for innovations, and a written rule for innovations produced by teams. Until those exist, neither figure is published: a conversion rate built on ambiguous identifiers would carry more confidence than the data supports.

What is still open

Open items
  • The duration is unsettled. Different figures are attached to different programmes in different years, and they may describe genuinely different programmes rather than one that shortened. They are not averaged, and no single “it takes N months” figure is published until it can be attached to a named programme.
  • The conversion rate is not yet known. Until it is, no claim about how well this pathway performs overall is available, only that its individual parts are evidenced.
  • Drop-out is under-modelled. People leave from every stage, and not enough is known yet about why. Every stage now records leavers and reasons; the answers are not in yet.
  • The bridge has a gap. Someone can finish the Maker Journey and not yet be ready for a multi-year venture pathway. A holding route, alumni projects, workshops, assistant-maker roles, exists to catch that, and it is a readiness gap, never an age gap.
