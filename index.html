DreamSpace Academy

Co-Creating The Dreams

DreamSpace Academy is a non-profit social enterprise in Sri Lanka. It works with communities on local social, economic and environmental problems, through education, innovation and entrepreneurship. This site explains how the whole model works, and how good the evidence is for each part of it.

About the organisation The theory of change

What this site is

This is the public reference for DreamSpace Academy: the organisation, the education system inside it, and the MakerSpace where that education runs.

It is written for educators, partners, funders and the communities the academy works with. It separates what is known, what has been seen here, and what is still being tested.

Education and the MakerSpace

Both sit inside that model. The education system is how capability gets built. The MakerSpace is where it gets built. Both are below.

The education system

The four stages of the learning journey

The journey keeps two questions apart: what a piece of work is for, and how deep it goes in one subject.

01

Explorer

Try materials, tools and ideas in a welcoming, low-pressure environment. Guided, and not assessed.

02

Maker

Build reliable capability through sustained, assessed projects in a chosen subject.

03

Innovator

Apply that capability to an open problem that exists outside the classroom.

04

Changemaker

Turn tested work into sustained contribution to a community.

The education standard

The four commitments

A stage describes purpose, and is named rather than numbered. A level describes depth inside one subject, and belongs to a programme rather than to a person. Completion needs evidence against a published standard, not attendance. And placement is by what someone can demonstrate, so prior learning counts however it was gained. The education approach sets out each one.

What the research says

Constructionism makes the case for learning by building things other people can see and use[1, 4]. Systematic reviews find that makerspaces support creativity, across a large body of studies[24, 25]. And assessing maker work is well precedented rather than experimental[21]. The reference library lists every source this site draws on.

The MakerSpace

The space this education runs in

The learning system above runs inside a community makerspace and digital-education hub. One space takes people from making, to innovation, to enterprise. The ecosystem is what surrounds those stages: peers, mentors, partners and funders.

The four verbs

Learn, Make, Solve, Earn. There is good evidence for the first three in maker education. There is not for Earn, which is why it is measured rather than claimed.

The purpose

To put the capability to solve local challenges into local hands, and the conditions that keep it there.

Mission

Co-creating with underserved communities the capability to keep solving their own challenges, through education, innovation and entrepreneurship.

Vision

Communities solving their own challenges.

The goal: one hundred local challenges solved by local people, by 2050. Counted against a published definition of a solved local challenge, not against the number of workshops delivered.

Taking part

Joining, visiting or working with the academy
