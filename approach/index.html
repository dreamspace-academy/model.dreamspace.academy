Education guide

The education approach

A learner-centred approach connecting knowledge, making, reflection and action, and what it fixes and what it leaves open.

Version 4.1.0 Published 27 August 2026 Updated 3 September 2026

Core definitions

Four distinctions give the system a common language for how learning and assessment are organised. They do not cover everything. Safety, inclusion, accountability to the community and what is actually running are separate questions, and they have their own pages.

Stage is purpose

Explorer, Maker, Innovator and Changemaker describe what the making is for. Stages are named, never numbered, and never graded against one another.

Level is programme depth

A level describes capability within one subject and attaches to a programme, not to a person, see where depth lives.

Making has no prescribed loop

A learner may begin from an aim, a material, an idea, a sound, a question or an accident. No single making sequence is imposed. A facilitator may teach or demonstrate a process where it helps, but following that process is not what is assessed.

Completion has a fixed standard

Assessed work must contain all six Definition of Done evidence states, in any order. This is the one thing that does not flex.

What is fluid, and what is fixed

Keeping these two apart is the main design decision. It lets each subject work in the way that suits it, while holding all assessed work to one comparable standard. A cycle that starts with “identify a problem” is shaped for engineering, and it fits music and much of design badly.

Deliberately fluidFixed
What it governs The making process, how a learner gets from nothing to something The completion standard, what must exist before assessed work counts as done
Why A prescribed cycle that opens with “identify a problem” is engineering-shaped, and fails for music, art and much of design Without a shared standard, a certificate from one facilitator does not mean the same as one from another
In practice Facilitators sequence sessions around the subject, the stated learning outcomes, learner readiness, access needs and safety Aim, Options, Made, Evaluated, Shared, Refined: all six, in any order

The six states are evidence categories, not steps. They may appear in any order, repeat or overlap, and the standard is never published, taught or drawn as a cycle. A loop implies an order the standard does not require, so that is the one way of presenting it that is ruled out. A facilitator may still walk a group through one possible way of working. What is assessed is that all six are present, not the route taken. Full detail: assessment.

One more boundary matters. Explorer work is not assessed at all. It runs as open, curiosity-led tinkering rather than against a set task[5]. Unassessed does not mean unguided. Facilitators keep the work safe, show what the tools can do, answer questions and give feedback when a learner wants it. None of that counts towards a certificate. The Definition of Done applies exactly where certification applies, from the Maker stage upwards. Assessing exploration would discourage it.

Placement is based on demonstrated prerequisites

Every programme provides a recommended route, so a beginner always knows where they are. That route is a recommendation, not a queue.

  • Where a programme allows direct entry, a learner demonstrates the prerequisites through a placement check rather than repeating learning they already hold.
  • Prior learning counts regardless of where it was acquired, at school, at work, online, or from a relative who repairs things.
  • Time enrolled does not by itself move anyone forward. Progression rests on demonstrated capability, completed work and the prerequisites of what comes next.
  • Eligibility for a stage transition is condition-based, not time-served. When someone actually starts still depends on programme dates, available places and supervision.

The placement check exists so that age, school year and prior enrolment are not used as stand-ins for readiness. It asks a learner to demonstrate the prerequisites directly. It does not capture everything: confidence, communication and access needs, recent practice and the support someone will want are considered alongside it, not inside it.

It matters most where the content really does depend on an earlier concept. Research on how people learn economic ideas shows that understanding profit rests on first grasping price comparison[45], and that saving and selling are accessible far earlier[43]. Those studies report their findings by age because they studied school cohorts. What they identify is a prerequisite, so the prerequisite is what gets tested.

Access and inclusion

Two commitments, both of which cost something.

Age does not determine placement

Age is not used as an entry rule, a band, a level label, or a published “typical age”. Where a learner starts is decided by what they can demonstrate. Age-related information is still used for the things it genuinely governs, safeguarding, legal duty of care and supervision, and a programme may carry practical participation requirements. A published typical age tends to be read as a rule.

Agency transfers to the learner

Participation and belonging in making environments improve when learners hold genuine decisions and when non-traditional ways of knowing are valued[27]. That is a design instruction: real choices inside the work, and a range of ways in. It does not mean the learner must supply the starting point: a project may begin with a facilitator, a community, a partner or a brief, provided the learner has genuine room to interpret and shape what happens next.

There is also a plain material dimension. In Sri Lanka, national statistics record computer literacy at 19.1% nationally, with the Eastern Province at 10.7% against 31.4% in the Western Province[58]. Provision that assumes a learner has a computer at home selects for the learners who least need it. International guidance reaches the same conclusion, technology in education has to be judged on whose terms it is introduced[52].

A note on terminology, because it causes real confusion. In this system PBL means project-based learning. Problem-based learning is a separate and well-established tradition with a different history and structure[16]. This model does not build on it. That is a choice about this design, not a judgement on the method. Where problem-based learning comes up, it is written out in full, because the abbreviation is not shared.

The strongest objection to this approach

Any education system that leans on discovery and open-ended projects has to answer a serious and well-argued objection: that minimal guidance during instruction does not work, particularly for beginners, and that teaching through problem-solving succeeds only once learners already know enough to solve problems[17].

That objection is largely correct, and this system is built to take it seriously rather than argue with it.

  • Guidance is highest where knowledge is lowest. Explorer work is facilitated tinkering rather than unsupported discovery. In the Maker stage, taught content and defined briefs establish capability early, and the learner takes more responsibility for defining and directing the work as that capability grows, the exit condition is a project they chose and carried themselves.
  • The solution space widens as scaffolding decreases, and only in that order, the pattern described in maker and technology education research, where a first workshop is narrowly defined and later ones give a bigger solution space with less support[15].
  • Structured teaching is a first-class delivery mode, not a fallback. See facilitation.
  • Open-endedness is present at every stage; what changes is the stakes and the support. Explorer work is open and low-stakes; Maker work pairs taught capability with widening choice; Innovator and Changemaker work carry that capability into problems and commitments beyond the exercise. What changes in the later stages is the consequence, not the openness.

The disagreement in the research is not really “discovery versus instruction”. It is about when. The answer used here is that the amount of guidance should follow what the learner already knows, which is also why placement measures that directly.
