Where an answer is “not yet”

A few of these do not have a settled answer yet: external accreditation is the clearest example, and long-term livelihood evidence is another. Those answers say so, and say what DreamSpace Academy is doing about it.

About the organisation

What is DreamSpace Academy?

DreamSpace Academy is a non-profit social enterprise that co-creates an ecosystem to tackle local socio-economic and environmental challenges through education, innovation and entrepreneurship. It was founded in Batticaloa, Sri Lanka, in September 2018.

About the organisation

Is “social enterprise” a claim that you do not rely on grants?

No, and it is not an admission of dependence on them either. The phrase describes a structure, not a funding position. The work has been funded by grants and partnerships, by revenue from the trainings the academy provides and from client projects by its media lab and software lab, and by the founders’ own capital. The relative shares have never been measured, so no ranking between them is claimed in either direction.

How the work is funded

Where does education fit in the wider model?

It is one of three pillars, alongside innovation and entrepreneurship, and it is the front of the whole system: it is how capability gets built before anything else can happen with it. The STEAM programmes are one part of it; the academy also runs business and entrepreneurship, digital security and teacher training. It runs in schools, at partner sites, online and in the academy’s own spaces. A MakerSpace is one of the places it can run, not the place it belongs to.

How many labs and ventures do you have?

Neither number is published. A lab is named only when it has a making domain, a named maintainer, a team and a date the statement is true as of; a venture count would need a stated role and a stated population it is counted from. Until those conditions are met, a figure would be misleading.

What part of your model is not proven?

Whether this particular rural pathway produces durable income. That entrepreneurship contributes to economic development is well evidenced; whether this maker-to-venture route does the same, and at what rate, is a hypothesis, and the measurement work exists to test it.

How well each part holds

Which Sustainable Development Goals do you claim?

Nine, each with something specific carrying it. Two came off the list recently: one was removed because the evidence did not fit the goal’s own definition, and one is held as stated intent because the outcome has never been measured. Both reasons are published.

What this is

What is MakerSpace by DreamSpace Academy?

It is a community makerspace that a local partner can set up and run as a social franchise. The partner provides the space; DreamSpace Academy provides the design of the unit (the equipment specification and layout), the brand, the operating standard and a licence to run its programmes there. The programmes do not need one: they also run in schools, at partner sites and online. Wherever they run, people learn by making things, working on projects and real local challenges, explaining and reflecting on their work, and taking on more of it independently over time.

The MakerSpace model

Is it a school, a training centre, or a makerspace?

The STEAM programmes borrow from all three and are none of them exactly. They are not a conventional classroom and not a computer lab. Learners work with tools, materials and other people, explore interests, build real capability and work on problems that exist outside the room they are in.

What a MakerSpace does not do ,A makerspace is not a computer lab

How is this different from a normal classroom?

In the STEAM programmes, learners do not mainly listen and repeat. They make things, test whether they work, explain the decisions behind them, improve them, work with other people, and apply what they know to projects and challenges that have not been solved for them in advance.

Facilitation

What are the STEAM programmes?

The academy’s programmes in making, robotics, electronics, fabrication, and music and media making, including its programmes in government schools. Learners learn by designing, building, testing, repairing and explaining tangible work. Technology is one possible tool in that, never the point of it: a well-made piece of furniture, a recorded song and a working sensor all qualify. The STEAM programmes are one part of what DreamSpace Academy teaches, not the whole of its education. In the research literature, this way of learning is called maker education.

Joining

Who can join?

It depends on the course, the places available, safeguarding requirements and any stated prerequisites. Open Explorer experiences need no prior skill at all; structured courses use a placement check.

Delivery formats

Is there a minimum or maximum age?

Placement is based on demonstrated capability, not age: age is not used as an entry rule, a band or a label anywhere in the system. Safeguarding and legal duty of care still apply, and an individual course may have practical participation requirements.

Why age does not determine placement ,Placement instead of age

Does a learner need good school grades?

Usually not. What matters is what a learner can already do and what support they need. Where a structured course has prerequisites, a placement check tests for those directly rather than using school results as a proxy for them.

How placement works

How do you decide which level a learner starts at?

A placement check looks at demonstrated knowledge, skill and independence. Prior learning counts however it was acquired (at school, at work, online, or from a relative who repairs things) and it does not need a certificate behind it.

What a level is

What you can learn

What does DreamSpace Academy teach?

The STEAM programmes: electronics, robotics, digital fabrication, music and media making, including term-based programmes in government schools. Beside them, business and entrepreneurship trainings, startup bootcamps and a startup challenge; media and information literacy, digital security and digital marketing; and workplace English, leadership workshops, training of trainers and training for school teachers. Software and AI are taught as well, and whether they belong inside the STEAM programmes or beside them has not been decided.

What the academy teaches

What subjects do the STEAM programmes cover?

The STEAM programme domains currently documented are maker and fabrication, digital education, AI programmes, AI with electronics, software and tech-entrepreneurship, creative arts, and fitness and sport. Software and AI also overlap with the academy’s digital skills training, and where they belong has not been decided. This is the current portfolio rather than a fixed structure: domains may be added, combined or retired. They are not all at the same stage of development, and the programmes page says which is which rather than presenting them as equal.

Which domains are offered now ,Where each domain stands

Does everyone have to learn coding or robotics?

No. A learner may come in through craft, music, media, art, sport, coding, electronics or another available area, and those practices overlap, with technology supporting work across any of them. DreamSpace Academy keeps the domains under one roof because real problems are not single-subject and people arrive through different doors.

The Changemaker Journey

What are Explorer, Maker, Innovator and Changemaker?

The four stages of the Changemaker Journey, the progression used in the STEAM programmes, describing what the work is for. Explorer is open discovery; Maker builds dependable skill in a chosen subject; Innovator aims that skill at a real problem; Changemaker is reached by producing work that came from a community, has been tested by a real user who is not on the project team, and that someone depends on.

What each stage is for

Is everyone expected to become a Changemaker?

No. The stages describe possible routes, not a ladder everyone must climb. Leaving with a real skill, a finished project or a job is a complete outcome, and the system is designed so that every stage records its leavers and where they went, rather than counting only the people who stayed. That record is not yet in place.

Where learners go next

What is a “level”?

A level belongs to a course, inside the Maker stage. It is three things at once: a description of capability, an expected volume of learning, and an assessable piece of work. It is never a label attached to a person: there is no such thing as “a Level 3 learner”.

What a level is ,What a level means

How long does the journey take?

There is no single duration, and none is published. It depends on the course, what a learner already brings, the projects they choose and the evidence they produce. Progress is based on demonstrated capability, not on time enrolled.

Assessment and certificates

How are learners assessed?

From the Maker stage onward, work is judged against a published Definition of Done: six evidence states that must all be present, in any order. Certification additionally requires three things: the artifact, a talk-through of it, and a reflection, which may be written, spoken or recorded. Explorer work is not assessed.

Assessment

Are there exams and marks?

The emphasis is on demonstrated work rather than a written exam or a single mark. A learner shows what they made, explains how it works and why they made the decisions they did, and says what they would change.

How quality is judged ,How maker learning is assessed

What does a certificate prove?

It names the course and level completed and the evidence used to judge it. It represents demonstrated work against a published standard, not attendance, which is why the standard is published.

Certificate evidence

Are the certificates nationally or internationally accredited?

Not as a system, and this is not yet settled. Unless a particular course names an accrediting body, a certificate should be understood as issued by DreamSpace Academy itself.

Trainers, facilitators and safety

Who teaches?

Trainers and facilitators, and one person is often both. A trainer is a content expert who teaches knowledge and skills directly and demonstrates safe practice. A facilitator is a process expert who coaches, asks questions, helps a group reach its own answers and connects people to others who know more. The balance changes as a learner becomes more capable. Guidance is highest where knowledge is lowest; structured teaching is a first-class part of this, not a fallback.

What a trainer and facilitator do

How are learners kept safe around tools and equipment?

Every course publishes its tool, material and safety requirements, and access to equipment is matched to demonstrated readiness and supervision. Safety requirements limit what a course may offer.

Quality and safety

What happens when a learner struggles, or something fails?

Trainers and facilitators treat failure as information, as long as safety holds. They can add instruction, demonstration, practice, feedback or peer support, and work can be revised before it is assessed. One of the six evidence states is Refined.

Outcomes, evidence and impact

Will this help a learner get a job or start a business?

It builds hands-on, digital, creative, innovation and enterprise capability, and enterprise runs through the whole journey rather than being added at the end. Small income is a realistic near-term outcome. A sustained livelihood is a hypothesis the academy intends to measure rather than claim, and the follow-up that would measure it is not yet running. Nobody is promised a job, a business or an income.

What “Earn” claims

How do you choose which community problems to work on?

The problem has to come from the community, not from a competition brief or a funder’s theme. Learners investigate the situation with the people affected, test whether the response is useful to a real user who is not on the project team, and a prototype on a bench is never counted as a solved problem.

What counts as a solved local problem

How can a donor or partner tell whether this works?

The academy reports activities, outputs, outcomes and long-term impact separately, so that each can be read for what it is. Limitations are published alongside results, and the academy treats documented curriculum, actual delivery and independently verified evidence as three different claims.

Measurement

What is this model’s biggest limitation?

There is no strong causal evidence that makerspaces improve long-term livelihoods in settings like this one, not in the academy’s own record, and not in the literature reviewed for this site. The system is built to generate that evidence rather than to assume it, and the instruments for doing so are published.

Taking part

What is running, where, and at what cost changes, so those answers come from DreamSpace Academy directly.

How do I find out what is running now, and where?

Courses, locations and dates change, so the current timetable is not published here. For what is open now (including places, dates, language of delivery, any fees or funded places, and accessibility arrangements) contact DreamSpace Academy at info@dreamspace.academy or through dreamspace.academy.

What should I ask before enrolling?

Useful questions: which course and level, what it costs and whether funded places exist, where and when it runs and in which language, what the placement check involves, what tools and materials are provided, what the safeguarding and supervision arrangements are, and what a learner leaves with. A course that cannot answer these is not ready to be offered, and every one of them is part of the published course specification.

What every course publishes

Is there support for learners with a disability, or who need another language?

Accessibility and language needs are handled per course rather than by a blanket policy, and they are part of what a course has to state before it is offered. Raise the specific need when you enquire. It is a practical question about the room, the tools and the facilitator, and it is better answered before enrolment than after.

How can a parent, school, donor or community organisation get involved?

By asking what is running now, visiting where that is appropriate, proposing a school or community partnership, contributing expertise, equipment or materials, or funding access and delivery. The route for all of these is info@dreamspace.academy.

Can this model be run somewhere else?

The programmes do not need a MakerSpace: they run in schools, at partner sites and online. A local partner that wants a dedicated space can set up a MakerSpace and run it as a social franchise, rather than as an unrelated space sharing a name. The academy provides the design of the unit, the brand, the operating standard and a licence to run its programmes there. What must stay identical is the completion standard, the stage definitions, the safety rules and the assessment method; the domains, language, timetable and problems worked on are set locally.
