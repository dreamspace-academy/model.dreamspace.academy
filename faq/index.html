About the organisation

What is DreamSpace Academy?

A non-profit social enterprise that co-creates an ecosystem to tackle local socio-economic and environmental challenges through education, innovation and entrepreneurship. It was founded in Batticaloa, Sri Lanka, in September 2018.

About the organisation

Is “social enterprise” a claim that you do not rely on grants?

No — and it is not an admission of dependence on them either. The phrase describes a structure, not a funding position. The work has been funded by grants and partnerships, by revenue earned through services and trading, and by the founders’ own capital. The relative shares have never been measured, so no ranking between them is claimed in either direction.

How the work is funded

Where does education fit in the wider model?

It is one of three pillars, alongside innovation and entrepreneurship, and it is the front of the whole system: it is how capability gets built before anything else can happen with it. The MakerSpace is the place that building happens in.

How many labs and ventures do you have?

Neither number is published. A lab is named only when it has a making domain, a named maintainer, a team and a date the statement is true as of; a venture count would need a stated role and a stated population it is counted from. Until those conditions are met, a figure would be misleading.

What part of your model is not proven?

Whether this particular rural pathway produces durable income. That entrepreneurship contributes to economic development is well evidenced; whether this maker-to-venture route does the same, and at what rate, is a hypothesis, and is why the measurement work exists.

How well each part holds

Which Sustainable Development Goals do you claim?

Nine, each with something specific carrying it. Two came off the list recently, one removed because the evidence did not fit the goal’s own definition, and one held as stated intent because the outcome has never been measured. Both reasons are published.

Impact and the global goals

What this is

What is MakerSpace by DreamSpace Academy?

It is a community makerspace and the learning system that runs inside it. People learn by making things, working on projects and real local challenges, explaining and reflecting on their work, and taking on more of it independently over time.

The MakerSpace model

Is it a school, a training centre, or a makerspace?

It borrows from all three and is none of them exactly. It is not a conventional classroom and not a computer lab. It is a space with tools, materials and people, in which learners explore interests, build real capability and work on problems that exist outside the building.

What it is not ,A makerspace is not a computer lab

How is this different from a normal classroom?

Learners do not mainly listen and repeat. They make things, test whether they work, explain the decisions behind them, improve them, work with other people, and apply what they know to projects and challenges that have not been solved for them in advance.

Facilitation

What does “maker education” mean?

Learning by designing, building, testing, repairing and explaining tangible work. Technology is one possible tool in that, never the point of it, a well-made piece of furniture, a recorded song and a working sensor all qualify.

Why making belongs in education ,Technology is a means

Joining

Who can join?

It depends on the programme, the places available, safeguarding requirements and any stated prerequisites. Open Explorer experiences need no prior skill at all; structured programmes use a placement check.

Delivery formats

Is there a minimum or maximum age?

Placement is based on demonstrated capability, not age, age is not used as an entry rule, a band or a label anywhere in the system. Safeguarding and legal duty of care still apply, and an individual programme may have practical participation requirements.

Why age does not determine placement ,Placement instead of age

Does a learner need good school grades?

Usually not. What matters is what a learner can already do and what support they need. Where a structured programme has genuine prerequisites, a placement check tests for those directly rather than using school results as a proxy for them.

Placement

How do you decide which level a learner starts at?

A placement check looks at demonstrated knowledge, skill and independence. Prior learning counts however it was acquired (at school, at work, online, or from a relative who repairs things) and it does not need a certificate behind it.

What you can learn

What subjects are available?

The domains currently documented are maker education and fabrication, digital education, AI programmes, AI with electronics, software and tech-entrepreneurship, creative arts, and fitness and sport. This is the current portfolio rather than a fixed structure: domains may be added, combined or retired. They are not all at the same stage of development, and the site says which is which rather than presenting them as equal.

The domains offered now ,Where each domain stands

Does everyone have to learn coding or robotics?

No. A learner may come in through craft, music, media, art, sport, coding, electronics or another available area, and those practices overlap, with technology supporting work across any of them. The domains sit under one roof deliberately, because real problems are not single-subject and people arrive through different doors.

The learning journey

What are Explorer, Maker, Innovator and Changemaker?

The four stages of the journey, describing what the work is for. Explorer is open discovery; Maker builds dependable skill in a chosen subject; Innovator aims that skill at a real problem; Changemaker is reached by producing work that came from a community, has been tested by someone outside the space, and that someone depends on.

The four purposes

Is everyone expected to become a Changemaker?

No. The stages describe possible routes, not a ladder everyone must climb. Leaving with a real skill, a finished project or a job is a complete outcome, and every stage records its leavers and where they went, rather than counting only the people who stayed.

Leaving and returning

What is a “level”?

A level belongs to a programme, inside the Maker stage. It is three things at once, a description of capability, an expected volume of learning, and an assessable piece of work. It is never a label attached to a person: there is no such thing as “a Level 3 learner”.

What a level is ,What a level means

How long does the journey take?

There is no single duration, and none is published. It depends on the programme, what a learner already brings, the projects they choose and the evidence they produce. Progress is based on demonstrated capability, not on time enrolled.

Assessment and certificates

How are learners assessed?

From the Maker stage onward, work is judged against a published Definition of Done, six evidence states that must all be present, in any order. Certification additionally requires three things: the artifact, a talk-through of it, and a written reflection. Explorer work is deliberately unassessed.

Assessment

Are there exams and marks?

The emphasis is on demonstrated work rather than a written exam or a single mark. A learner shows what they made, explains how it works and why they made the decisions they did, and says what they would change.

Judging quality ,How maker learning is assessed

What does a certificate prove?

It names the programme and level completed and the evidence used to judge it. It represents demonstrated work against a published standard, not attendance, which is why the standard is published.

Certificate evidence

Are the certificates nationally or internationally accredited?

Not as a system, and this is genuinely unsettled. Unless a particular programme names an accrediting body, a certificate should be understood as issued by DreamSpace Academy itself.

What this does not solve

Facilitators and safety

Who teaches?

Facilitators, whose job changes as a learner becomes more capable: they teach directly, demonstrate safe practice, coach, ask questions, and connect people to others who know more. Guidance is highest where knowledge is lowest, structured teaching is a first-class part of this, not a fallback.

What a facilitator does

How are learners kept safe around tools and equipment?

Every programme publishes its tool, material and safety requirements, and access to equipment is matched to demonstrated readiness and supervision. Safety is a design constraint on what a programme may offer, not a poster on a wall.

Quality and safety

What happens when a learner struggles, or something fails?

Failure is information, and it is treated that way, as long as safety holds. A facilitator can add instruction, demonstration, practice, feedback or peer support, and work can be revised before it is assessed. One of the six evidence states is literally Refined.

Outcomes, evidence and impact

Will this help a learner get a job or start a business?

It builds practical, digital, creative, innovation and enterprise capability, and enterprise runs through the whole journey rather than being added at the end. Small income is a realistic near-term outcome. A sustained livelihood is something the academy measures rather than claims, nobody is promised a job, a business or an income.

What “Earn” claims

How do you choose which community problems to work on?

The problem has to come from the community, not from a competition brief or a funder’s theme. Learners investigate the situation with the people affected, test whether the response is useful to someone outside the space, and a prototype on a bench is never counted as a solved problem.

What counts as a solved local problem

How can a donor or partner tell whether this works?

Activities, outputs, outcomes and long-term impact are reported separately, on their own lines, so that each can be read for what it is. Limitations are published alongside results, and documented curriculum, actual delivery and independently verified evidence are treated as three different claims.

Measurement

What is this model’s biggest limitation?

There is no strong causal evidence that makerspaces improve long-term livelihoods in settings like this one, not in the academy’s own record, and not in the literature reviewed for this site. The system is built to generate that evidence rather than to assume it, and the instruments for doing so are published.

Taking part

This site is a reference, not a prospectus, what is running, where and at what cost changes, and those answers come from DreamSpace Academy directly.

How do I find out what is running now, and where?

This site describes the system rather than the current timetable, because programmes, locations and dates change. For what is open now (including places, dates, language of delivery, any fees or funded places, and accessibility arrangements) contact DreamSpace Academy at info@dreamspace.academy or through dreamspace.academy.

What should I ask before enrolling?

Useful questions: which programme and level, what it costs and whether funded places exist, where and when it runs and in which language, what the placement check involves, what tools and materials are provided, what the safeguarding and supervision arrangements are, and what a learner leaves with. A programme that cannot answer these is not ready to be offered, and every one of them is part of the published programme specification.

What every programme publishes

Is there support for learners with a disability, or who need another language?

Accessibility and language needs are handled per programme rather than by a blanket policy, and they are part of what a programme has to state before it is offered. Raise the specific need when you enquire. It is a practical question about the room, the tools and the facilitator, and it is better answered before enrolment than after.

How can a parent, school, donor or community organisation get involved?

By asking what is running now, visiting where that is appropriate, proposing a school or community partnership, contributing expertise, equipment or materials, or funding access and delivery. The route for all of these is info@dreamspace.academy.

Can this model be run somewhere else?

That is the intention, as a social franchise rather than a set of unrelated spaces sharing a name. What must stay identical is the completion standard, the stage definitions, the safety rules and the assessment method; the domains, language, timetable and problems worked on are deliberately local.
