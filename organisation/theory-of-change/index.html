Theory of change

How the work is meant to produce the result: four streams rather than one chain, the layer where the best-evidenced mechanisms sit, and a grade on every claim.

The chain in one line

Build the local conditions, capable people, a place to build, the money, market access, introductions and continued support that turn skill into a livelihood, and the network that connects an underserved community to the world, and measure whether it works.

Each clause answers one of the conditions set out under why this work exists. A deliberately sceptical search for evidence on the chain from rural maker education to durable income found none, so measuring it is part of the work.

The four streams

The work does not run as a single pipeline. What gets delivered runs in four parallel streams, over a shared research foundation, converging on a medium-term layer before anything that deserves to be called impact.

Each stream is stated the same way, inputs, activities, outputs, short-term outcomes, medium-term outcomes, impact, and each carries its own grade for how well the evidence supports it. They are not equally strong.

Capability

The education pillar: term-based ICT and robotics inside government schools as a designed multi-year engagement, the learning journey through Explorer, Maker, Innovator and Changemaker, and training for teachers, principals and preschool teachers alongside makerspace and partner bootcamps.

The output is learners with assessed work that meets a single completion standard, and a delivery system capable of teaching the next cohort. The short-term outcome is skills, digital inclusion and self-efficacy, and a school able to deliver its own mandated syllabus. The medium-term outcome is a local delivery workforce: alumni teaching the cohort behind them.

The system strategy

The training does not stop with children. It runs from children to teachers, then principals, then preschool teachers. That is a capability transfer into the state’s own delivery layer: the gap it fills is well evidenced, and the state has already committed to the mandate.

Innovation & venture

The innovation and entrepreneurship pillars: lab work with challenge-based learning running through all of it, the DreamSpace Lifecycle, and the four support modes, co-creation, bridging, domain expertise and funding, drawn on in any combination and any order.

The outputs are grassroots innovations and launched ventures. The short-term outcome is an innovation adopted by a real user outside the makerspace, and small income from self-made products. The medium-term outcome is ventures still trading at twelve and twenty-four months, local employment created by alumni ventures, capital recycling through the loan fund, and founders who come back to mentor.

Three different claims about income

Small income from selling self-made products is a modest, low-risk claim: making produces sellable output. Sustained livelihood is a different claim entirely, and it covers a wage as much as a venture. Work created for others is downstream of both and is currently unmeasured here. There is no employment count. Only the first of the three is safe to state plainly today.

Bridging & system

Being the node, and connecting it. Global-to-local bridging, building physical hubs, state partnership, hosting researchers, and giving local people a stage in front of visiting delegations. Earlier versions of this model listed the partner and funder network as an input. It is an activity rather than an input.

The outputs are connections made, exposure placements, hosted researchers, and funding that reached other organisations rather than this one. The medium-term outcome is becoming the first partner anyone entering the region talks to, and the local organisations nearby being stronger for it. Research on entrepreneurial ecosystems is consistent on this: what produces entrepreneurial activity is the surrounding conditions, not a single programme delivered into a place with none of them[34, 35].

Social mission

The conditions under which any of the rest is possible: peacebuilding and social cohesion, media and information integrity at scale, humanitarian response, work with women and girls, disability inclusion, and interfaith practice across five religions since 2018. Measured by delivery volume this is the single largest cluster of work, and it has historically been the most under-claimed.

The medium-term outcome is the licence to operate, the community trust and institutional standing that make everything else possible at all.

What the evidence supports

The evidence does not support “education and enterprise create peace”. It supports something narrower and more useful: that the structural distribution of educational opportunity is itself a driver or dampener of conflict; that non-formal, locally-rooted provision reaches marginalised youth where formal schooling does not[65, 68]; that integrated, sustained programmes outperform single-purpose ones; and that neither peace nor training produces livelihoods on its own.

The most defensible structural claim sits here. Formal and non-formal provision each fail at what the other reaches, and the literature argues for combining them[66]. The academy runs both: inside government schools and outside them. That is a claim about position rather than outcome.

Research as an output

The research foundation is not a support function on this model. It is a stream-level output in its own right: an evidence base, a case-study and pattern register, the honesty rules, peer-reviewed output and a hosted doctoral study. The search for evidence on this chain found none, so producing it is one of the outputs. The ask is to fund the work and the evidence.

How well each part holds

Making builds skills, creativity and inclusion Well evidenced Supported across the maker-education literature[27, 24].

The route people take is not the model

This is often compressed into one arrow: maker education, then challenge-based learning, then grassroots innovation, then entrepreneurship, then an impact venture. That sequence is a route some people take. It is not the model, and the academy's own case record says so.

Across the documented cases, exactly one is a full sequential traverse from the first stage to the last. Entry happens at every point, some people arrive already innovating, some arrive through the labs, and one arrived because of work he had already published himself. The theory is best evidenced at its destination and weakest at its conversion.

Why that is published

The conversion step, a motivated candidate becoming somebody who has produced a tested solution, is the least evidenced stage in the model. It is named here so that it can be worked on.
